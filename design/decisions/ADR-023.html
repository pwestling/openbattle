ADR-023: Rolling alone

Status
Proposed Best guess made on 2026-10-15 while Porter slept, at his request; easy to reverse. Awaiting Porter.
Context
Under ADR-022, a roll or a draw from a shuffled deck needs a value signed by a player who is not the host. A host playing alone, setting up before the other player arrives, or whose opponent has dropped out, could then never roll or draw from a shuffled deck.
Decision
When no other player is connected, the host can still roll or draw from a shuffled deck alone. The host's browser makes both values itself, and the action records that nobody else took part. Everyone, at the time and later in the log and in saved files, sees the roll or draw marked "rolled alone", which is not tamper-evident. With another player connected, the sealed exchange is always used.
Kept fair by Session
The log does not say who was connected, so the record cannot stop a host going alone while an opponent is there. Session keeps it fair in three ways: the host's browser goes alone only when no other player is connected; a player's browser flags any roll or draw taken alone that arrives while it is connected; and a player who signed a value that the host never logged is told so, since a host could otherwise ask for values until it liked one.
Because
Playing solo, testing a list or setting up a table should not be blocked. Marking the roll keeps it honest: nobody can pass off a roll made alone as a fair one.
Rejected
Refusing to roll alone: blocks solo play and setup. Letting a spectator provide the other value: a spectator is not in the game, and could be a second browser of the host's own.
Affects
Roll, Deck, ADR-008, ADR-022, Record surface, Session surface, Screen surface
