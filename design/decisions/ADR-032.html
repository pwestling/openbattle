ADR-032: A model's place is one value, and a target says what it is

Status
Accepted From findings 3 and 6 of the decomplecting review, 2026-10-17, before any code builds reserves, markers or items on the old shapes. Accepted by Porter on 2026-10-17.
Context
A model is kept as "set aside, yes or no" beside a position that means nothing while it is set aside, and every reader has to check the flag first. ADR-025 adds reserves, kept apart from removed models, which a yes or no can't hold. A counter or mark names what it sits on by a bare id, or the word "game", and the record finds out what that id is by looking in every list in turn; ADR-025 and ADR-027 add terrain, markers and items to that list.
Decision
A model's place is one value: on the table (with its position, facing and floor), in reserve, or removed. Only a model on the table has a position. A counter or mark names its target together with what kind of thing it is: a model, unit, group, player, terrain piece, marker or item, or the game itself. The table keeps counters and marks for each kind of thing apart.
Because
Each fact is stored once, and nothing reads a stale position or guesses what an id names. Cards already keep their place this way. It is cheap now and costly once the rules package reads counters and reserves everywhere.
Rejected
Keeping the yes or no and adding a second "reserve" flag beside it: two flags that must agree. Keeping bare ids: every new kind of thing that can hold a counter grows the guessing.
Affects
Model, Counter, Mark, Record, Measure, Screen
