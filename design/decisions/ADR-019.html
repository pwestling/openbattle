OpenBattle design · Level 1 · Decision

ADR-019: Terrain and models can be meshes loaded from files

Status
Proposed From Porter's surface review on 2026-10-15, extended to models.
Context
Real scenery and miniatures have shapes no box or cylinder captures, and players already have meshes for both, for example from Tabletop Simulator. For many games a model's shape is not just looks: its height, and what it can see and be seen by, follow from it. The surfaces only allowed simple solids and stand-ins.
Decision
A terrain piece is either a simple solid or a mesh, and a model's body is either a mesh or a simple stand-in of a given height. Meshes come from .glb, .gltf or .obj files of at most 512 MB (so loading one can't crash a browser tab), each stored once however many models use it. A mesh is the true shape for measuring heights, and later for sight lines. The log names a mesh by the fingerprint of its file's bytes. The mesh reaches the other browsers either as the file itself, sent browser to browser through the session, or as an https link to where it can be downloaded, such as S3, which each browser downloads and checks against the fingerprint. Each browser keeps its copy with the game, and a saved game file includes it. There is no physics. When a piece is placed, Measure works out a few flat floors from its mesh and the place action keeps them, so every browser agrees. A model stands on whichever floor under it the player picks, never balances or falls, and passes through walls freely; whether passing through matters is left to the players or a later rules package.
Because
The fingerprint guarantees everyone draws the same shapes and keeps the log small, whichever way the file arrived. Links spare the players from sending large files over their own connections. Neither way puts the file on our server, which stays out of the game, as ADR-006 requires.
Rejected
Links as the only identity of a mesh: they break or change. With the fingerprint as the identity, a changed link is caught instead of quietly showing different scenery. Uploading meshes to the server: the server would store game content. Simple solids and stand-ins only: neither looks nor measures like the real thing. Meshes as decoration over a separate simple shape: heights and sight would disagree with what players see. Resting models on the mesh with physics: models would tip, slide and disagree between browsers. Working floors out in every browser on load: tiny numeric differences could give browsers different floors.
Affects
Terrain, Model, Measure, Session, Archive, Session surface, Archive surface
