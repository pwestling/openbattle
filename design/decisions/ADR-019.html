OpenBattle design · Level 1 · Decision

ADR-019: Terrain and models can be meshes loaded from files

Status
Proposed From Porter's surface review on 2026-10-15, extended to models.
Context
Real scenery and miniatures have shapes no box or cylinder captures, and players already have meshes for both, for example from Tabletop Simulator. For many games a model's shape is not just looks: its height, and what it can see and be seen by, follow from it. The surfaces only allowed simple solids and stand-ins.
Decision
A terrain piece is either a simple solid or a mesh, and a model's body is either a mesh or a simple stand-in of a given height. Meshes come from .glb, .gltf or .obj files of up to 25 MB, each stored once however many models use it. A mesh is the true shape for measuring heights, and later for sight lines. The log names a mesh by the fingerprint of its file's bytes. The file travels browser to browser through the session, is kept with the game in each browser, and is included in a saved game file. Models set down on a terrain mesh rest on its surface.
Because
The fingerprint guarantees everyone draws the same scenery and keeps the log small. Sending files between browsers keeps the server out of the game, as ADR-006 requires.
Rejected
Links to meshes on the web: they break, change, or differ between browsers. Uploading meshes to the server: the server would store game content. Simple solids and stand-ins only: neither looks nor measures like the real thing. Meshes as decoration over a separate simple shape: heights and sight would disagree with what players see.
Affects
Terrain, Model, Measure, Session, Archive, Session surface, Archive surface
