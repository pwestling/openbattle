The screen's outside is the person using it. Each section is a place or a gesture. Mouse and keyboard controls follow Tabletop Simulator's where it has one.

Start page

Shows
New game (your name, colour, table size, 60 × 44 by default) and Open a saved file.
Does
New game opens the table as host, with the invite link ready to copy. A file opens as a new game starting from that log.
Fails
If the server can't be reached, says so and offers to retry.

Arriving from a link

Shows
Your name and colour with Join, or Watch. Someone returning on the same browser skips this and goes straight back to their seat.
Fails
A broken link goes to the start page with a message.

The table

Camera
Tabletop Simulator's camera controls (ADR-017): right-drag or the arrow keys turn around the table, middle-drag or W A S D slide, the wheel zooms, and Z or a middle-click zooms to the pointer and back again. P looks straight down and back again, and Space goes back to your table edge; T (straight down) and H (your edge) do the same. The camera is yours alone. It has no bound on how far it slides or zooms out (ADR-020), since Space always brings it home.
Controls
Where Tabletop Simulator has no control, the screen picks one (ADR-017), and lists every control on screen under Controls.
Picking up
Click a model to select it; click again or double-click to select its whole unit; Shift-click adds a model or takes it out; drag a box to select several; or click a unit's name in the Army panel to select its models on the table. Dragging lifts the selection off the table and carries it, keeping its shape; Q and E turn it. It is carried at the floor under the pointer, so pointing at a roof carries it onto that roof. Letting go sets it down: one move action. Escape puts it back where it was. A model standing over a marker is always picked before the marker; a marker is picked by clicking where no model stands on it, and dragged like terrain (ADR-025).
While carrying
A line from where each model started shows the inches moved, live. Pressing Space (or a right-click) pins a corner, so a path around terrain is measured along its true length. A model already moved this turn is measured from where it began the turn, through the corners pinned before: dragging it again adjusts its move rather than adding to it, and a leg is added only by pinning a corner. Once there are corners, the label shows the leg being moved, with the whole way as a second line. A new turn begins when the round or whose turn it is changes; until the first such change, the whole game so far counts as one turn. The line is drawn thick and pale over a dark edge, the same for every player, and nothing lingers on the table after the move.
Ruler
Hold Tab, while the table has focus, and drag between two spots, or from a selected model, pinning waypoints the same way. Shows inches across along the whole path, and the height difference, in the same pale line as a move. Private; nothing is sent.
Sight
With models selected, hold V while the table has focus and point at a model or a spot, on the table or on a piece of terrain, a wall's side included. Each selected model gets a line to it: pale where it can see, red where it can't. The label says how much is seen ("Sees 60%" or "Can't see", or "3 of 5 see" for several models), and the pieces hiding part of the target are outlined, with their names shown in red. Holding Shift as well leaves models out of the way, so only terrain blocks; clicking an outlined piece leaves it out until V is let go. Private; nothing is sent. Until every mesh on the table has reached this browser, it names the pieces still missing instead (ADR-028).
Models
The base at its true size with the model's mesh on it, or a simple stand-in body in the player's colour where there is no mesh, and a small label, shown once the pointer has rested on the piece for half a second, and not while a measurement is showing, so a crowded table stays readable. A piece whose mesh file hasn't reached this browser yet shows its stand-in, labelled as missing its mesh, until the file arrives. When adding to an army, a mesh file can be chosen for each unit or model.
Undo key
Ctrl-Z (Cmd-Z on a Mac) undoes your own latest action, exactly when the log's Undo would, whether or not the log is open; held down, it undoes once. While typing in a text field, it is the field's own undo.
Fails
If a move is refused (host away, or a problem), the models settle back where they were and a short note says why.

Dice tray

Shows
How many dice, how many sides (six by default), an optional label, and Roll. Recent rolls stay in the tray.
Does
Dice are thrown onto the tray and land on the decided result, for everyone at once, then line up sorted, each turning square and upright as it lines up. A d4, d6, d8, d10, d12 or d20 is that real shape; any other number of sides is drawn as the closest of them, with the result on top. A d4 shows its result on the face turned up, standing on its point. The dice and sides fields can be emptied to type a new number; Roll waits until both hold one. A tampered roll is shown in red with a warning. A roll made alone, with no other player connected, is marked "rolled alone" in the tray and the log, in a colour of its own, since it is not tamper-evident. Being alone never makes it show as tampered; a roll that is both shows both marks, in tampered red (ADR-023).

Decks and hands

Shows
Decks sit on the table as face-down stacks with a count; a shuffled deck is marked as such. Your hand is a row of cards along the bottom of the screen; everyone else's hand shows as card backs with a count. Face-up cards lie on the table where they were played, as thin slabs with an edge and a patterned back, so a stack has height. A deck's, pile's or card's label shows on a moment's hover, as a piece's does.
Does
Click a deck to draw into your hand, or Shift-click to draw face up. Drag a card from your hand, or on the table, to play it onto the table, onto a discard pile beside its deck, or back into a deck (onto the top, or holding Shift the bottom, of an ordered one). F or a middle-click flips the card under the pointer, as in Tabletop Simulator; it turns over up and over, so everyone sees it flip. Shuffle on an ordered deck makes it shuffled. A new deck is made in the Decks panel from a typed or pasted list of cards (Name: text, one per line), with optional pictures, and starts ordered as listed or shuffled; it sits just off your table edge. A tampered draw is shown in the log in red with a warning, as a tampered roll is. A shuffled draw made alone is marked "drawn alone" in the log, as a roll made alone is (ADR-023).
Fails
An empty deck says so and draws nothing.

Panels

Phase bar
Round, whose turn, phase name; each can be changed by a player. Beside it, who has priority and its note, in their colour; the players who have it see "Your move". Hand over gives it to the other player, to both or to nobody, with an optional note (ADR-026).
Army
Each player's units and groups with their counters and marks. Change a counter with + and −, toggle a mark, set models aside or return them. Add army, in the top bar, opens a dialog where you paste or type a list to add units or models to your army, at the start or at any point in the game; it closes once they are added, and a list being typed is kept while the game is paused: one unit per line, such as 5x Scouts 25mm, Captain 40mm 1.5" or Tank 100x60mm rect 2.5" (count, base, and the stand-in's height; by default 1, a 25 mm round base and 1.2″). The x after a count may be left off (10 Scouts); a line with no name, such as a lone number, is reported as unread. A list exported as text from Army Forge reads too: each unit line (Battle Brothers [5] Q3+ D3+ | …) is that many models on the default base (2x Name [5] is one unit of ten), and the equipment lines under it are skipped. A line naming one of your units adds models to it. New models stand in rows along your table edge. Opening a model shows the items it carries, nested as they hold each other, each with its counters and marks, and lets a player add, rename, move to another model or remove one (ADR-027). To reserve sets a unit aside into reserve, listed under Reserve apart from Removed; returning it stands it along its owner's table edge, ready to place (ADR-025).
Terrain
Add a box, cylinder or ramp, or load a 3D mesh file (.glb, .gltf, .obj or .stl), then size, move and turn it like a model. Lock a piece, or Lock all, so it can't be picked up, selected or labelled on the table and models are carried over it freely; Unlock or Unlock all to move it again. For now a lock is kept by this browser alone.Any player may lock or unlock any piece; a lock is an action, so everyone sees it, the log says so ("Ana locked Box"), it survives a reload, and it can be undone. A piece being carried when someone else locks it is put back. New pieces go in a row across the middle of the table. Each mesh format is read as its makers mean it: glTF in metres, STL in millimetres with +z up, and OBJ, which says neither, in inches with +y up. A model set down over terrain lands on the highest floor under it; while carrying, Page Up and Page Down (or Shift with the wheel) step between the floors there. Models pass through walls; nothing pushes them away. Each piece shows its counters and marks, such as Cover or Difficult, changed as a unit's are (ADR-025).
Markers
Beside Terrain: add a disc or a rectangle with a name, a size in inches and a colour. It lies flat on the table, belongs to nobody and can be dragged, turned and resized by anyone. Its counters and marks are shown and changed as a unit's are. Remove takes it off the table (ADR-025).
Log
Opened and closed with Log in the top bar; it starts closed, and closing it while looking back returns the table to now. Every action as a sentence ("Ana moved Scouts 5.8″"). A slider looks back through the game; the table shows that moment, read-only, until you return to now. Undo appears beside your own latest action. From Record's undo rule, it appears only while your action is the latest one in the game not yet undone, because undo cancels that one. Once anyone acts after you, your action can no longer be undone. It never appears beside a roll or a draw from a shuffled deck, which the record never undoes, nor beside a join: leaving the game is not an undo. An action's note, if it has one, shows after its sentence ("Ana marked Scouts Shaken. Failed morale: rolled 2, needed 4+"), and a roll's label is its note (ADR-029).
Decks
Beside Army and Terrain: make a new deck, and Draw, Draw face up or Shuffle each deck.
Save
Saves the game to a file. Available to players and spectators.

Rules

Shows
Rules in the top bar, saying No rules or the package the game uses, such as Grimdark Future. With a package on, its suggestions for you appear as small buttons hovering over the units they are about, such as Activate over each of your units that hasn't gone yet, facing the camera. Clicking one opens it into the next choices in place: Activate opens into Hold, Advance, Rush and Charge. While an action is open, its reach is drawn lightly on the table around the unit: how far it moves and how far each weapon type shoots, labelled ("Rifles 24″"). Suggestions with no unit, such as End round, sit in a Next step strip above the dice tray, which also says what the game waits on from the other player ("Waiting on Ben: roll 7 blocks, 4+"). Spectators see the strip without buttons.
Does
Choosing a package, or No rules, turns it on or off for the game for everyone, as an action. To shoot, the screen asks for a target for each weapon type in turn ("Rifles at…"): enemy units in range and sight get a Target button over them, and clicking the unit itself picks it too. Once two different units are picked, only those two can be picked for the rest, as the rules allow. Picking is yours alone until the dice are rolled; Escape or clicking elsewhere backs out one choice. The last step of a choice takes it: its actions go through as yours, each carrying its words as a note, or its dice are thrown in the tray as an ordinary roll with them as the label. Anything can still be done by hand, and the suggestions follow what the table then shows. With a package on, Add army reads the list through it, so units arrive with their stats as counters, marks and items, and lines it can't read are listed as today (ADR-029, ADR-030).
Fails
If a suggestion's action is refused (host away, or a problem), nothing is taken and a short note says why, as for a move.

Connection banner

Shows
Nothing while all is well. "Connecting…", or "Waiting for the host. The game is paused" with the table read-only, or "Your table differs from the host's" with a button to reload the host's copy. It also says when this browser won't keep the game, so it can't be picked up again from here.

When the screen breaks

Shows
If anything on the screen fails, a page saying something went wrong, with what broke and a Reload button, instead of a blank page. If this browser keeps the game, reloading comes back to the same table.
